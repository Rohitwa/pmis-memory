# Craft rules (stable)

These are the slow-changing writing rules. Apply all of them when drafting the line and the note. They are separate from the fast-learning resonance map.

1. **Frame from THEIR viewpoint, not your savings.** The hook is their vision / mandate / metric — not "we cut your cost." Demote cost to a single supporting clause or keep it as proof for the call.

2. **One message = one value-prop.** Never layer two offers (e.g. support + collections) in one note. If two angles fit, pick the one on their top mandate.

3. **Outcome-priced language.** Prefer "per resolved case / per rupee recovered / variable cost that scales with X" over "cheaper." This is the language a CFO/economic buyer trusts.

4. **Two-beat opener cadence** (works for cost-heavy pains):
   *"You've built [impressive system] — but it takes [big cost / N people]. We convert that into [per-outcome] that [flows to the bottom line]."*

5. **Reframe the gap as their unfinished journey**, not a deficiency you're poking at. Easier to say yes to.

6. **Public data only. Flag estimates as estimates.** Never state a triangulated number as if it were reported. Never quote their complaint/failure stats *at* them.

7. **Greenfield beats already-built.** If they already run a solution like yours, it's a displacement sale — say so and pitch only the open lane, or self-park.

8. **Recovery/collections often beats support-deflection** in value. When a business has a high-frequency outbound pool (EMI, deposits, renewals), lead with recovery, not desk-cost.

9. **Match register to the archetype.** CFO → variable cost / unit economics. Founder-CEO → speed / scale / "before you scale." Procurement → route-to-sponsor + low-risk pilot. CX-practitioner → show-don't-tell / live proof. Ops → uptime / scale-without-headcount.

10. **Tie urgency to their growth.** "As X scales to Y, this gets worse" — so doing nothing has a cost to their plan, not just their P&L.
