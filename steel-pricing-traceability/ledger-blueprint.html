Metal Cost Ledger Blueprint

One system that traces the metal, proves the money, and moves the money — for component makers squeezed between market-priced inputs and index-priced outputs.

The principle

Every leak in the chain — untraceable part costs, disputed claims, unfinanceable receivables — has one root: verifying reality at part level was always too expensive, so the industry runs on proxies (indices, average costs, balance sheets). AI agents collapse the cost of verification. Nothing new is created; the paperwork that already exists becomes an asset.

The core asset — a per-part ledger

A continuously maintained genealogy for every part number shipped:

part ← BOM metal content ← purchase lots (heat no.) ← actual landed cost, net of actual scrap

Set against customer price circulars and index publications, it yields the one number nobody has today: actual vs reimbursed metal cost, per part, per month — with the gap decomposed into timing lag, grade basis, recovery factor, and yield drift.

How it is built

  • Ingestion agents read the documents where the truth already lives: mill invoices, test certificates, POs and goods receipts, BOMs and drawings, price amendment letters, scrap sale invoices.
  • Reconciliation agents map purchase lots to parts through BOM and consumption records — and flag gaps, grade substitutions, and yield drift for human review rather than guessing.
  • No system integration to start. Documents in, ledger out. ERP hooks come only after the value is proven.

What the ledger powers

Claim

Price-adjustment claims auto-assembled in each customer's format, every line backed invoice-to-shipment. Settled fully and fast instead of negotiated late and partially. A person approves every submission.

Cash

The proven claim becomes bankable: a financier advances against it in days and carries the wait, repaid when the amendment lands. Ledger-valued stock unlocks the same for inventory — including mill consignment, pay-on-consumption.

Terms

Every clause — index choice, reset frequency, recovery factor, scrap convention — back-tested against price history to show its cost per year. Evidence in hand at the next renewal, plus a pass-through score lenders can price credit on.

Rollout

  1. Metal margin audit — 4–6 weeks, one quarter of documents in, a per-part leakage number out. Proves the value before any platform commitment.
  2. Continuous ledger — live leakage tracking and claim automation as a running system.
  3. Finance layer — claim and inventory financing with partner institutions; extend the same mechanics one tier down the chain.

Guardrails

Agents propose, people submit — no claim leaves without human sign-off. Each company's data stays in its own deployment. Accuracy is the product: one wrong claim costs more than a hundred right ones earn.
