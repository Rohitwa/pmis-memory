Three routes to the AI Accountant website

The source document is a complete pitch — product, proof, security, pricing, team. A website shouldn't carry all of it. Each route below picks one reader, one job, one narrative and keeps only the content that serves it. The three routes are deliberately incompatible with each other: choosing one means choosing what the site is for.

What the document gives us

Twelve distinct content blocks. No route uses more than seven at full strength — the rest get compressed to a line or cut entirely.

Curation rule for every route: a section earns its place only if the target reader would act differently after reading it. Everything else is one line, a footnote, or gone.

Route A
Proof-ledConversion page

The Audit

"We found $53,000 of missing tax in 36 invoices. Nobody had caught it."

ledger paper · ink · audit red · settled green · rule beige
Type: Libre Caslon display · IBM Plex Mono numerals

Reader & job

CFOs and finance heads who believe their books are fine. The page has one job: create the itch to run the agent on their own files. It sells the audit finding, not the software.

Tone

Forensic, understated, numeric. Short declarative sentences. The page looks like a ledger: ruled hairlines, tabular numerals, red flags on found items. No product screenshots — evidence instead.

Why this framing

The document's strongest asset is the live-client findings — they are specific, verifiable-feeling, and alarming. Leading with the product invites comparison shopping; leading with "here's the money you're losing right now" invites a trial. The 2–3% leakage stat becomes the villain of the whole page.

Page flow — single scroll, one CTA repeated

HERO / FINDING"In 36 payment files we found a bill about to be paid twice, $53,000 of missing tax, and a vendor billing 150 licences for 85 agreed." CTA: Run it on your last month's invoices.
THE LEDGERThe four findings set as audit lines — date, item, amount, flag. Interactive: hovering a line reveals what the agent saw. Closes with "That was 36 documents. You process thousands."
THE LEAKWhy this happens: the work is slow, so mistakes are found after money leaves. 2–3% of payables, quantified against the visitor's own spend via a small slider calculator.
HOW IT CATCHESPipeline compressed to three beats: reads every document, matches everything continuously, flags before payment. No agent taxonomy here.
PROOF BARClient names + 12 / 110 / $100M / $3M stat strip. Shriram Group lineage as one line of small print.
THE COMMITMENTS"What it is not" reframed as five signed commitments — ERP stays master, maker–checker on every entry, your data trains only your deployment.
PRICE & CTA$6,000 + $29/seat stated plainly next to "$3M saved to date." Final CTA repeats the hero offer with phone + email.

Kept at full strength

  • Live results — promoted to hero + own section
  • Leakage stat — becomes an interactive calculator
  • What it is not — reframed as commitments
  • Pricing — transparency signals confidence
  • Client names + stats — one proof bar

Compressed or cut

  • Six-agent taxonomy — cut; internal architecture, not a buying reason here
  • PRISM-ES stack — one trust line + link; the acronym is for security reviews, not first contact
  • Founder bios — cut to names in the footer
  • Full pipeline diagram — compressed to three beats
  • Roadmap — one line: "live in three weeks"
Tradeoff: strongest at generating trials, weakest at satisfying a controller doing due diligence — it deliberately withholds the how. Needs Route B's material available one click deeper for the second visit.
Route B
Product-ledSystems walkthrough

The Machine Room

"Your ERP finally does the work" — a living schematic of the pipeline, running on screen.

graphite · bone · signal green · panel slate · annotation grey
Type: Archivo display · IBM Plex Mono schematic labels

Reader & job

Controllers, finance ops leads, and IT evaluators — the people who will be asked "will this actually work with our SAP?" The job: survive due diligence. Answer every hard question before it's asked.

Tone

Technical confidence. Dark-first schematic aesthetic: an animated document flowing through the six stages in the hero, monospace annotations, precise numbers (99%, 90–95%, 5–10%) rendered as instrument readouts.

Why this framing

The document's pipeline diagram is genuinely good — it already thinks like a systems page. This route makes it the hero: a document arrives (email · PDF · scan · WhatsApp), gets read, coded, matched, and posted, live, on scroll. Evaluators trust what they can trace.

Page flow — anchored sections, sticky nav

HERO / SCHEMATICThe 6-step pipeline as a scroll-driven animation. Headline: "An accountant made of software. It sits on the ERP you already run." ERP logos (SAP, Oracle, Tally, Zoho) as input sockets.
THE SPLIT70/30 rendered as an honest diagram: what the agent takes (routine), what stays human (judgment), and the handoff — exceptions arrive with documents, history, and a suggested action. One click.
THE SIX AGENTSFull agent table as spec cards — Reader, Entry, Reconciliation, Flagging, Reporting, Decision — each with its one job and one number. "Onboarded like an employee: show it your documents and rules once."
SECURITYPRISM-ES at full depth, read bottom-up as the doc intends. The plain-language summary line — "the AI sees only what the signed-in person can see" — set large; the seven layers expandable beneath.
ROLLOUTThree-week roadmap as a timeline: Week 1 shadow-run and compare books entry-by-entry, Week 2 full AP/AR at real volume, Week 3+ group-wide. "A switch, not a project."
BOUNDARIES / FAQ"What it is not" verbatim as an FAQ — this is exactly the section finance teams ask for.
PRICING & CONTACT$6,000 + $29/seat/agent/month with a worked example (e.g., 5 seats × 3 agents). CTA: Book a technical walkthrough.

Kept at full strength

  • Pipeline — promoted to animated hero
  • Six agents — full spec-card treatment
  • PRISM-ES — only route that uses all 7 layers
  • Roadmap — full 3-week timeline
  • What it is not — verbatim, as FAQ

Compressed or cut

  • Live findings — compressed to stat chips inside relevant sections ($53K tax sits next to the Flagging agent)
  • Founder bios — cut entirely
  • Leakage narrative — one framing line in the split section
  • Client roster — logo strip only
Tradeoff: most credible and most complete, but coldest — it assumes the visitor already wants the category. Weak at creating urgency; a CFO skimming for 20 seconds leaves without feeling the leak.
Route C
Narrative-ledBrand story

The New Hire

"Meet your new accountant. Starts in three weeks. Works nights. Never forgets."

cool paper · navy ink · marigold · workaday blue · pencil grey
Type: Fraunces-class warm serif display · humanist sans body

Reader & job

Founders and CEOs of mid-market companies — people who feel finance headcount as a monthly pain, not a process diagram. The job: make the product feel like a hiring decision they already know how to make.

Tone

Warm, plainspoken, a little wry. The document already talks this way — "onboarded like a new employee," "it never forgets," "nobody types." This route commits to the metaphor and carries it through every section as an employment story.

Why this framing

Software framing invites an IT evaluation; hiring framing invites a gut decision. Every hard concept maps cleanly: agents become a team with roles, results become references, "what it is not" becomes terms of employment, pricing becomes a salary — $29 a month against the cost of a person.

Page flow — one continuous story

THE OFFERHero as an offer letter: "Meet your new accountant." Three lines of terms: works 24×7 · learns your rules once · every dollar checked before it leaves. CTA: Interview the agent.
THE WORK NOBODY WANTSThe four daily jobs — typing, checking, reporting, chasing — written from the team's point of view. "The first three take the most people, the most hours, the most money."
WHAT THEY TAKE OFF YOUR PLATE70/30 as a promise about people, not percentages: "your team stops typing and moves to the 30% that needs a brain." Same function, ~30% of the people.
MEET THE TEAMSix agents as résumé cards — name, role, one-line specialty, one stat. The Flagging agent's card carries the $53K catch as its proudest moment.
REFERENCESLive results told as short anecdotes ("at one client, it stopped a bill from being paid twice — in its first week") + client names. 12 companies, $3M saved.
TERMS OF EMPLOYMENT"What it is not" as contract clauses: never posts without a check, never touches the master record, never shares what it learns. Security condensed to one plain sentence.
START DATE & SALARYThree-week onboarding plan, then pricing as compensation: $29/seat/agent/month. Sign-off from the hiring managers — Mohit & Rohit, the one route where founder credentials appear.

Kept at full strength

  • Employee metaphor — from a single doc line to the whole spine
  • Four daily jobs — the emotional opening
  • Six agents — as characters, not specs
  • Founders — only route that features them; a hire needs a hiring manager
  • Roadmap — as an onboarding plan

Compressed or cut

  • Pipeline diagram — cut; replaced by a one-line "day in the life"
  • PRISM-ES — cut to one sentence inside the terms
  • Accuracy percentages — mostly cut; metaphor beats metrics here
  • Implementation fee detail — folded into "start date" as a simple line
Tradeoff: most memorable and most shareable, but the metaphor is a commitment — it can read as soft to a skeptical CFO, and enterprise buyers (Govt. of Odisha-scale) may want the machine room, not the story. Metaphor discipline required: one wink per section, never a costume party.

Recommendation

Lead with Route A, back it with Route B. The findings are this company's rarest asset — no competitor can copy "$53,000 of missing tax in 36 files," but anyone can draw a pipeline. The Audit converts the first visit; the Machine Room's sections (agents, PRISM-ES, roadmap, FAQ) live one click deeper as /how-it-works and /security for the evaluation that follows.

Route C is worth keeping in the drawer: its "new hire" voice is the strongest copy system of the three, and lines from it ("nobody types," "it never forgets") can season Route A without committing to the full metaphor.
