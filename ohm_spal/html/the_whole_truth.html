0. Identity check

The Whole Truth Foods is the brand of FITSHIT Health Solutions Pvt Ltd, Saki Vihar Road, Andheri East, Mumbai. Founded by Shashank Mehta (ex-Hindustan Unilever) with co-founder Rachna Aggarwal; last day at Unilever 9 July 2019, first sale 19 October 2019. No ambiguity with another company of the same name.

Read-through: revenue tripled while the loss grew only ₹4 cr, so operating leverage is real. But the business is still spending ₹1.15 to earn ₹1, operating cash burn was ₹88 cr against a closing cash pile of ₹141 cr, and the Series D (roughly ₹430 cr at the time) arrived with "profitability" and "IPO" as the stated next steps. The gap from −13% EBITDA to break-even is the whole story for the next 24 months, and it has to be closed while whey, the input behind half the revenue, has roughly tripled in cost.

A structural note on revenue [S]: quick commerce platforms in India run an inventory-led model, buying from the brand at a margin. The brand's reported revenue is therefore already net of the platform margin on roughly 40% of sales, which is why reported marketplace fees are only ₹9 cr. The ~39% gross margin should be read as a blended figure across channels with very different realisations.

2. Sector baseline: structural P&L levers for a premium clean-label D2C food brand in India

Scope: a ₹200–500 cr venture-funded food brand selling high-protein and clean-label products to affluent urban consumers across quick commerce, marketplaces, own site and early offline, with a mix of own and contract manufacturing. Levers are organised by P&L line.

3. Fallback rule applied: where sector assumptions substitute for company evidence

Items tagged [S] carry no company-specific evidence and should be confirmed in discovery:

  1. Revenue recognition net of QC platform margin (affects how gross margin and P1 are read).
  2. Pack-size and grammage changes (P3).
  3. Promotion and discount funding (P4).
  4. Wastage and expiry rate (C5).
  5. Inventory days and split between own plants and contract manufacturers (W1).
  6. Whey purchase terms and any hedging (W3).

Company facts that would materially change the picture and are worth confirming early:

  • Gross margin by category: powder vs bars vs others. If powder is half of revenue at a lower margin, the whey problem is bigger than the blended 39% suggests.
  • Actual QC take rate and visibility spend by platform, and what the ₹9 cr marketplace line covers.
  • Current channel mix (the 40/25/25/10 figure is from January 2026).
  • Inventory and packaging write-down from the label change.
  • Series D use-of-funds split between plant capex and working capital.
