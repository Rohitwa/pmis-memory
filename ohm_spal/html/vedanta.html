In plain words

Vedanta is a London-owned, Mumbai-run mining and metals group that had its best year ever in FY26 and then split itself into five listed companies on 1 May 2026. Aluminium makes almost half the profit. Zinc and silver, through Hindustan Zinc, make the other large part. Oil and gas, power, iron and steel, and copper are smaller and each has a problem of its own.

Three things decide its bottom line, and none of them is a secret.

  1. Metal prices and the rupee. When aluminium and zinc prices rise and the rupee falls, profit jumps. FY26 was that year. Management cannot control either.
  2. Cost per tonne. This is what management can control, and it is where the group has done its best work: aluminium cost at a five-year low of $1,752 a tonne, zinc at $959. The levers are captive coal and bauxite mines, captive power, and plant efficiency. Several of those mines are still waiting for environmental clearance.
  3. Money that leaves the operating companies. The London parent, Vedanta Resources, carries about $4.5 billion of debt and no operations of its own. It is paid through dividends and a "brand fee" of about $400 million a year. In FY26 the two together were about $1.1 billion. Every rupee of that comes out of the Indian companies before minority shareholders or reinvestment see it.

What the books show underneath the record profit: suppliers fund the group (creditor days of about 64 against debtor days of 9), the FY26 profit includes ₹3,498 crore of exceptional charges and a ₹1,252 crore exploration write-off, finance cost was ₹8,351 crore, and the power business lost money in the first quarter after the split. The April 2026 boiler explosion at the Athena plant in Chhattisgarh killed 24 contractor workers and put the Chairman's name on an FIR. Twenty-four fatalities in one incident at a plant run by an outsourced O&M contractor is the operational finding of the year.

For S-PAL, the group is five different sales conversations. The most exposed P&L is Vedanta Power. The largest procurement and plant base is Vedanta Aluminium. The cleanest, most cash-rich is Hindustan Zinc, which sits inside the residual Vedanta Ltd.

People and scale [C]

Group permanent employees run to tens of thousands; the Iron & Steel entity alone discloses 3,685 permanent employees of which 3,013 are factory staff and only 97 in finance. Contract and subcontract labour is the larger workforce at every plant: the 24 people killed at Athena were all employees of the O&M contractor NGSL, an NTPC–GE joint venture. Group LTIFR 0.4 in FY26, TRIFR 1.3; fatalities had already risen from 7 (FY20) to 13 (FY23) and 7 (FY25) before FY27's incidents.

Read-through

The group's profit is high and its costs are falling, and both are true. Underneath, three structural things decide what the owner keeps: commodity prices he does not set, mine and permit timing he does not fully control, and a holding-company debt that takes $1.1 billion a year out of the operating companies before anyone else. The operational findings cluster in two places: outsourced O&M at power plants, where 24 people died, and procurement, where the group runs on 64 days of supplier credit across thousands of vendors and more than a dozen sites. Those two are where a controls conversation starts.

3. Fallback rule applied

Vedanta is one of the best-documented companies in India, so few levers are sector-assumed. The gaps that matter:

  1. Procurement spend, vendor count and the share through long-term contracts, by entity (C5). [S]
  2. Contract-labour headcount by plant and the O&M contract structure beyond Athena (C4). [S]
  3. The FY26 Vedanta Ltd auditor's report and contingent-liability note at group level; this page relies on the Iron & Steel memorandum and Screener's ₹31,673 cr. [S]
  4. Working-capital and payables by entity after the split. [S]
  5. The actual brand-fee amount by entity in FY26 (the CFO promised the number "after the call"). [S]

Facts worth confirming early, by entity:

  • Vedanta Power: Athena Unit 1 status, insurance recovery, PPA penalties, and whether the NGSL contract survives.
  • Vedanta Aluminium: Sijimali and Kuraloi EC dates; alumina captive share today; bauxite cost after the OMC reset.
  • Residual Vedanta Ltd: how the Union Bank facility secured on HZL shares is being unwound; copper exposure at Fujairah.
  • Vedanta Oil & Gas: Rajasthan infill plan versus the 90–95 kboepd guidance; exploration write-off policy.
