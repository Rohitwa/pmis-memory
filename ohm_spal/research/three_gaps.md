# The three gaps between what happens and what gets decided

A simplified evidence report. Prepared 28 Oct 2026 for the OHM narrative. Every number carries its source and a confidence grade: **A** peer-reviewed or primary filing, **B** industry body or large benchmark, **C** vendor or secondary citation of a primary source not checked directly.

## 1. The model in three lines

A company's system of record is wrong in three places, and the three errors compound.

1. **The recording gap.** Time passes between an event and its entry in the ERP. During that time the system is wrong by omission.
2. **The attention gap.** A person entering or checking a record can hold four or five checks in mind. The record is accepted with the rest unchecked.
3. **The decision gap.** The people who plan and decide have limited hours. Most of those hours go to assembling and cleaning data, and the decision waits for the review calendar.

The loss from any one decision is **value at stake × share decided late or wrong × cost per day of delay**. The three gaps set the "late" and the "wrong".

## 2. Gap 1: the recording gap, sized

| What is measured | Number | Source | Grade | What it does to the company |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Days from invoice receipt to posting, average organisation | 9.2 days | Ardent Partners, AP Metrics That Matter 2025, 212 AP teams | B | Payables, accruals and spend reports are more than a week behind reality |
| Same, best-in-class vs laggards | 3.1 days vs 17.4 days | Ardent Partners 2025 | B | The gap between best and worst is two and a half weeks of blindness on every bill |
| Calendar days to close the month, median | 6.4 days (6.0 in a 2026 reading of 10,198 organisations) | APQC Open Standards Benchmarking | B | The first trustworthy view of a month arrives a week into the next one |
| Same, top quartile vs bottom quartile | 4.8 days vs 10+ days | APQC | B | Bottom-quartile firms decide on numbers two weeks stale |
| Inventory records that are wrong | 65% of 370,000 records across 37 stores | DeHoratius and Raman, Management Science 2008 | A | Two of every three stock figures the ERP shows are not what is on the shelf |
| Size of the error when wrong | Average absolute deviation ~35% of the item's actual stock | DeHoratius and Raman 2008 | A | Replenishment, allocation and write-down decisions start from a number a third off |
| Items customers could not find that were in the building | 16% misplaced in the back room, not out of stock | Raman, DeHoratius and Ton, HBR 2001 | A | The system said "in stock"; the customer left |
| New records with at least one critical error | 47%; only 3% of datasets met the loosest standard | Nagle, Redman and Sammon, HBR 2017, 75 executives auditing 100 recent records each | A | Half of what is entered needs a person to fix it before it can be used |
| Supply-chain leaders with real-time visibility beyond tier three | Fewer than 25% | Gartner, cited secondhand | C | Most plans run on supplier data that is days or weeks old |
| Goods received physically but posted at shift end | Common practice, not quantified | Warehouse-software vendors | C | Stock exists for hours before anyone else can see it |

**Effects.** Retail out-of-stocks run at 8.3% worldwide (Gruen, Corsten and Bharadwaj 2002, grade B). Inventory distortion, out-of-stocks plus overstocks, costs retail 6.5% of global sales, $1.77 trillion in 2023 (IHL, grade B). Shrink runs at 1.6% of sales (NRF 2022 survey, grade B). Each of these is a recording gap that became a commercial loss.

|---|---|---|---|---|

**Effects.** The attention gap converts to leakage that is small per transaction and large in total: 1.5% of disbursements, 5% of revenue to fraud, a tenth of negotiated savings. None of these shows up as a line in the P&L. They are found at audit, at recovery, or never.

**Effects.** The decision gap is not that managers decide badly. It is that the decision is made from a picture that took weeks to assemble and is already old, by people who had a quarter of their time to think about it.

## 5. How the three gaps compound

Take one vendor bill in a mid-sized Indian manufacturer and follow it.

| Step | Typical lag | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Goods arrive, receipt posted at shift end or next day | 1 day | Recording |
| Invoice arrives, matched and posted | 9 days average | Recording |
| One in five bills raises an exception, resolved by hand | 7 days at bottom performers | Attention |
| Four or five checks performed; price variance, duplicate, MSME status, GST filing status mostly not | 0 days, permanent | Attention |
| Payables appear in the monthly close | 6 days after month end | Recording |
| Spend and margin reviewed at the monthly or quarterly meeting | 15 to 45 days | Decision |
| **Event to decision** | **30 to 70 days** | |

Across that window the money has left, the price has moved, the stock has aged, and the decision is a correction, not a choice. The six operating problems in the OHM narrative are this window seen from six angles: planning, resource allocation, inventory tracking, material planning, procurement risk and working capital.

## 6. Where each gap bites, by type of organisation

| Organisation type | Dominant gap | Why | Evidence from the accounts in this wiki |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process manufacturer with commodity inputs (metals, chemicals, foam) | Recording, then decision | Input prices move daily; stock cover and landed cost are known monthly; pre-buying is the only hedge | Wakefit: raw material doubled to ₹105 cr in nine months; margin "borrowed from old inventory"; bank stock statement and books disagreed three years running |
| Multi-site assembler with OEM price-variation clauses | Attention | Compensation depends on proving metal weight and lot cost per part; nobody holds that in their head | Auto Ignition: pass-through lagged one to two quarters and partial; receivables 75 to 100 days |
| D2C or omnichannel brand | Recording and decision | Stock sits in warehouses, stores, dark stores and platforms; sell-out is seen late; new SKUs launched faster than old ones are judged | Snitch: 5,000 SKUs, current assets 45% of revenue, two FY25 numbers in circulation; The Whole Truth: ₹60 cr working-capital gap found at year end |
| Project business (design-and-build, EPC) | Attention and decision | Thousands of vendor bills against a BoQ; scope changes paid to vendors but unbilled; margin known at closure | Flipspaces: its own job posting names "commercial controls around budgeting, billing, change orders, cost overruns and project closure" as the gap |
| Diversified group with outsourced operations | Attention | Plants run by O&M contractors and contract labour; thousands of vendors on 64 days of credit; five ERPs after a split | Vedanta: 24 contractor deaths at one plant; creditor days 64 vs debtor days 9 |

## 7. What the evidence does and does not support

**Supported strongly.** The recording gap is a week to two weeks on transactions and a week on the monthly picture. Stock records are wrong two times in three. Half of new records need fixing. A person misses a fifth to a third of what they check. One bill in five needs manual handling. One to two percent of payments go out wrong. Planners spend half their time cleaning data.

**Supported, but by vendor or secondary citation.** Duplicate-payment rates, maverick-spend rates, SKU-level forecast error, the Gartner cost figure. Use them with the grade shown, or confirm against the primary report before putting them on a slide.

**Not found in this research.** A study that measures goods-receipt posting lag directly. A published figure for excess-and-obsolete write-downs as a share of inventory by industry. An academic measurement of how many checks a person performs per transaction; the "four or five" in the model is an assumption supported by the inspection and data-entry literature, not a measured count.
